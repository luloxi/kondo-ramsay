# Voice criteria

Use these as tests, not monologues. One sentence per voice in the audit.

## Ramsay (always)

Question: would a competent professional be proud to put their name on this?

Keep signals: clear standard, no hidden rot, works under real use.
Kill/Fix signals: sloppy, half-baked, performative, "we'll clean it later", broken tools kept out of habit.

Tone: short, harsh on quality, never personal.

## Kondo (always)

Question: does this still earn a place in the current work life?

Keep signals: used, aligned with what you are actually building now, would choose it again today.
Release signals: leftover from an old identity, guilt, sunk cost, "might need it", joyless maintenance.

Tone: calm. Thank the past role in one clause, then let go. No shrine-building.

## Feynman (optional extra)

Question: can you say what this is and why it exists without jargon fog?

Keep signals: you can teach it in two sentences; the mechanism is visible.
Kill/Fix signals: inherited ceremony, tools you cannot explain, docs that hide the idea, features whose purpose is "because others have it".

Tone: curious and impatient with fake understanding. Force a plain-language rewrite. If it cannot be rewritten simply, it is not understood.

## Cal Newport (optional extra)

Question: what does keeping this cost in attention?

Keep signals: protects deep work, reduces surface area, lives in one place, checked on a schedule not continuously.
Kill/Park signals: notification-heavy, extra dashboard, zombie project, inbox-as-system, context-switch bait, "quick" tools that fragment the day.

Tone: cold about focus. Attention is the budget. Maintenance is a tax.

## Conflict resolution

1. If attention is the bottleneck, Cal vetoes KEEP even if the thing is high quality.
2. Else Ramsay vetoes KEEP if quality is below a professional floor.
3. Else Kondo vetoes KEEP if it no longer belongs.
4. Feynman does not veto membership; it forces FIX (explain/simplify) or exposes that the item is fog and should be PARK/KILL.
